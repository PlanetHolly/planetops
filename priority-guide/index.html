Priority Production How to apply it

This is the rush throttle for peak season. Standard price already covers a normal job; the priority charge funds the overtime a rush forces on one press. Use it every time a customer needs to jump the line.

1. When you can offer it

  • Order is $1,500 or more, AND
  • The customer needs it faster than our standard turnaround.

Under $1,500: no priority. Standard timeline only.

3. When the clock starts

The timeline starts the business day after the order is approved AND paid. Both have to happen before the production ticket begins.

4. Get Jean's sign-off first (do not skip)

Any priority that is 3 business days or faster, OR carries a 25% or higher charge, needs Jean's production approval before you confirm it to the customer. We cannot promise what the floor physically cannot do. 4-Day and 5-Day are your call.

5. Always ask for flexibility Every order

A standard on every order, not just rushes. Right after the order is paid and moves into production, send the flexibility note. It is saved in TextBlaze as /flexdate. Most dates that look firm have a little give, and the slack you surface gives production room to breathe.

One more thing as we move your order into production. I want to make sure it gets our full attention. If your due date is firm, we're already on it. But if there's any give in your timeline, even a few days, it helps us do our best work.
