The whole machine, one sentence

Job Time (min) = print rate × qty + (8 + 12 × colors)   ·   Cost/unit = Job Time ÷ 60 × $—/hr ÷ qty A job costs setup time plus per-piece press time, and every press hour costs a known amount. That is the entire model — everything on this page explains or feeds those two lines.

Setup is paid once per job (burning in, registration, ink-up), so it fades as quantity grows — that is the whole reason bigger runs are cheaper per piece. The per-piece time barely changes from 1 to 2 colors because bandanas don't double-pass; it jumps at 4+ colors, which is why 4+ goes to Bear Designz and is not priced by this curve.

What the curve deliberately does not know

  • No imprint-size dimension. The rate is keyed on colors only — a 16.5" and a 21.65" print cost the same here. Only 16.5" (small screen) is genuinely cheaper; 17"/19"/20" all run the big screen. The 27" bandana still carries 22" labor: open item.
  • 4+ colors is outsourced (Bear Designz) and priced by their invoice, not this curve. In-house cap: 3 colors.
  • DTF and heat press are excluded. This is the screen-print engine only.
  • The setup formula (8 + 12 × colors) is the least-measured input — bandana-informed but never cleanly timed. An audit against the Project Calculator's June–July actuals is queued to harden it.
  • The rate is a floor. The export minutes that produced the hours are known to overstate real press time, so the true rate is at or above the locked value.
